\title{LLMs Meet Cricket: Systematic Evaluation of Contextual Factors in Dismissal Prediction}


% ------------------------------------------------------------------
\begin{abstract}
Cricket ranks as the world's second-most popular sport with approximately 2.5 billion fans, yet predicting how a batter will be dismissed remains exceptionally challenging. Each dismissal type (bowled, caught, LBW, stumped, run‑out, etc.) is shaped by layered contextual factors such as venue, pitch, weather, opponent, toss, bowler style, field setup, and match state. Traditional predictive models, including Bayesian survival analysis, hidden Markov models, and random forests, often overlook critical context due to data sparsity, high-dimensionality, and operational constraints, leading to limited scalability and frequent retraining needs. While Large Language Models (LLMs) offer the ability to tailor predictions to each player through in-context learning, it remains unknown which contextual inputs maximize prediction accuracy. In this paper, we systematically evaluate LLMs for batter dismissal prediction by evaluating the impact of different contextual inputs. Through comprehensive ablation experiments using structured (career statistics, venue and opponent history) and unstructured (recent match commentary) contextual inputs across multiple LLM variants, we find that including venue and opponent context elevates top-1 accuracy from 43\% (career stats alone) to 57.5\%, and top-2 accuracy from 57\% to 79.2\%. Among tested models, o4-mini achieves the highest accuracy, while GPT-4.1-mini offers the optimal trade-off between accuracy and efficiency for practical deployment. Our results establish which contextual factors most enhance predictive performance, provide a scalable and interpretable LLM-based framework for cricket analytics, and outline clear directions for future work in sports prediction.
\end{abstract}

\section{Introduction}
Cricket is the second most popular sport in the world, with an estimated 2.5 billion fans spread throughout South Asia, the United Kingdom, Australia, and beyond. Despite its massive following and the wealth of statistics collected over decades, predicting how a batter will be dismissed remains challenging. Unlike the batting average or strike rate, which can be predicted with simple statistical models, the prediction of a dismissal in a match is significantly harder to model. It is shaped by a web of interdependent context-specific factors such as pitch conditions, weather, bowler–batter matchups, field placements, and the current game situation. Accurately capturing this interplay is essential for coaching strategies, insightful commentary, and tactful on-field decision-making.

Traditional machine learning models, such as Random Forests, Naive Bayes classifiers, Bayesian Survival Analysis, and Hidden Markov Models, have been widely applied in cricket analytics~\cite{cricket_analytics_survey,cricket_dismissal_traditional,sports_data_mining}. However, they face several fundamental limitations in cricket dismissal prediction. First, these models typically optimize for average-case performance, failing to capture the full complexity of rare but critical dismissal events. Second, they struggle with the inherently imbalanced nature of dismissal data, where certain outcomes (e.g., stumpings, run-outs) constitute less than 5\% of total dismissals yet carry significant strategic importance. Third, these static models require computationally expensive retraining cycles to accommodate temporal shifts in player performance, rule changes, and evolving match strategies. Finally, their reliance on predefined feature sets limits their ability to discover novel patterns or adapt to unprecedented game situations. These constraints collectively undermine both their predictive accuracy for edge cases and their practical deployment in real-time decision-making contexts.

Recent advancements in Large Language Models (LLMs) offer a compelling alternative~\cite{gpt4_evaluation}. LLMs excel at in-context learning, allowing them to incorporate both structured data (e.g., player career statistics, venue and opponent history) and unstructured data (e.g., recent match commentary) without the need for extensive retraining. This flexibility makes them particularly effective at modeling the situational complexity of dismissal events — capturing subtle patterns shaped by constantly evolving game conditions and player dynamics. However, a fundamental question remains unresolved: \emph{which contextual inputs most strongly drive LLM prediction accuracy?} Identifying these inputs is essential for building reliable, efficient, and interpretable sports analytics systems.

In this paper, we present the first systematic evaluation of LLMs for cricket dismissal prediction through a comprehensive ablation study. Our analysis draws on data spanning over 3,000 ODI matches, yielding more than 1,700 ball-by-ball commentary files, 600+ aggregated player records, and performance metrics across 100+ cricket grounds worldwide (see Section~\ref{sec:data-collection}). We analyze the predictive contribution of different contextual inputs across multiple LLM variants (e.g., GPT-4.1-nano, GPT-4.1-mini, GPT-4.1, o4-mini) and uncover three key insights: ($i$) incorporating match venue and opponent context proves decisive, boosting top‑1 accuracy from 43\% (career statistics only) to 57.5\% (+14.5pp), and top‑2 accuracy from 57\% to 79.2\% (+22.2pp); ($ii$) LLMs substantially outperform traditional models, with o4-mini achieving 57.5\% top‑1 and 79.2\% top‑2 accuracy, while GPT‑4.1‑mini provides 56.3\% top‑1 and 76.8\% top‑2 accuracy (Figure~\ref{fig:model-comparison}), delivering the most favorable trade‑off between predictive accuracy, computational cost, and deployment efficiency among the evaluated LLMs; and ($iii$) our LLM‑based framework is scalable and interpretable, adapting to evolving match conditions while providing transparent feature attribution for each prediction.

Together, these contributions address a challenging cricket problem while advancing predictive modeling in data-sparse, context-rich settings. By demonstrating where LLMs outperform traditional models, our work lays the groundwork for real-time tactical insights and next-generation sports intelligence.


\section{Related Work}

\subsection{Sports Analytics and Machine Learning}
The intersection of sports and machine learning has evolved significantly over the past two decades, with cricket analytics emerging as a particularly rich domain for predictive modeling. Traditional approaches have predominantly relied on statistical frameworks including regression analysis, survival models, and Bayesian inference to capture player performance patterns~\cite{cricket_analytics_survey}. Early dismissal prediction systems employed rule-based methodologies that encoded expert domain knowledge into decision trees, while more sophisticated approaches leveraged ensemble methods such as Random Forests and boosting algorithms to handle the inherent complexity of cricket data~\cite{cricket_dismissal_traditional}. Recent advances have explored computer vision techniques for cricket performance analysis~\cite{cricket_pose_estimation,cricket_backlift_recognition} and machine learning approaches for match outcome prediction~\cite{t20_match_prediction}.

Despite these advances, traditional ML approaches face key limitations when applied to dismissal prediction. Poor generalization remains a persistent challenge, as models trained on historical data often fail to adapt to evolving playing conditions, player form, and emerging tactical innovations. The high retraining frequency required to maintain predictive accuracy creates operational burdens that limit scalability in real-world deployments. Perhaps most critically, these approaches suffer from a lack of context; an inability to incorporate the rich situational factors that human experts intuitively recognize as decisive in dismissal outcomes~\cite{sports_data_mining}.


\subsection{Large Language Models in Sports}
The emergence of LLMs has opened new frontiers in sports analytics, with applications spanning automated commentary generation, strategic pattern analysis, and match outcome prediction~\cite{llm_sports_prediction}. LLMs bring three key advantages to sports prediction tasks: (1) natural language processing capabilities that enable direct integration of textual match reports and commentary without significant preprocessing, (2) contextual understanding that can capture complex non-linear relationships between heterogeneous data sources, and (3) in-context learning that allows adaptation to new scenarios without model retraining~\cite{contextual_learning_survey}.

Recent work has demonstrated the potential of LLMs in various sports contexts, from generating real-time commentary that rivals human experts to providing tactical insights that inform coaching decisions~\cite{llm_sports_prediction}. 
Prompt engineering enables direct encoding of sports-specific rules, statistical relationships, and analytical frameworks into model inputs~\cite{prompt_engineering_survey}, bypassing traditional feature engineering while preserving domain expertise.
%The \emph{prompt engineering} paradigm has proven particularly valuable, allowing researchers to encode domain-specific knowledge and analytical frameworks directly into model inputs~\cite{prompt_engineering_survey}. This approach circumvents many traditional limitations by leveraging the model's pre-trained knowledge while maintaining flexibility for domain-specific adaptation.

However, three challenges limit current applications, which remain largely unaddressed especially in cricket analytics.
First, we lack principled methods for effectively combining structured numerical statistics (e.g., player performance metrics) with unstructured textual match descriptions (e.g., match reports) in prompt design and evaluation methodologies.
Second, domain adaptation presents ongoing difficulties, as sports terminology and contextual nuances may not align with LLM training distributions.
Third, evaluation frameworks for sports prediction tasks lack standardized metrics, making it difficult to compare approaches and establish reliable benchmarks for progress.

\subsection{In-Context Learning and Predictions}

Modern LLMs demonstrate impressive capabilities in few-shot learning, where models adapt to new prediction challenges using only a handful of examples provided within the input context~\cite{few_shot_learning,gpt4_evaluation}. \emph{Chain-of-Thought (CoT) reasoning} has been shown to be particularly effective for complex prediction tasks, enabling models to divide intricate problems into manageable steps while maintaining interpretability throughout the decision process~\cite{chain_of_thought}.

Structured prediction, encompassing multi-class classification, sequential decision making, and constraint satisfaction, represents a natural application domain for LLM capabilities. The ability to incorporate textual context, temporal patterns, and domain-specific knowledge within a unified framework can address many limitations of traditional approaches. This integration is particularly valuable in sports analytics, where prediction accuracy often depends on subtle contextual factors that resist quantification through conventional feature engineering. While researchers have demonstrated LLM effectiveness in various sports contexts~\cite{cricket_win_prediction}, the specific contextual factors that maximize prediction accuracy remain largely unexplored.


\section{Problem Formulation}
We formally define our multi-class classification task as predicting the \emph{most likely dismissal mode} from eight distinct categories: bowled (ball hits the stumps directly), caught by fielder (ball is caught before bouncing), caught by wicketkeeper (ball is caught behind the stumps), stumped (wicketkeeper breaks the stumps while the batter is out of the crease), LBW (leg before wicket—ball would hit the stumps but strikes the batter first), hit wicket (the batter accidentally breaks the stumps), caught and bowled (bowler catches the ball directly), and not out (the batter remains unbeaten at the end of the innings).

This prediction challenge operates within a heterogeneous input space that encompasses both structured and unstructured data sources. Structured inputs include comprehensive career statistics (batting averages, strike rates, historical dismissal patterns), venue-specific performance records that capture ground-wise variations in playing conditions, and opponent-specific data reflecting team-wise matchup patterns. Unstructured inputs include rich textual information from ball-by-ball commentary, pitch condition reports, and strategic context describing field placements and tactical decisions.

The problem exhibits several characteristics that make it particularly challenging for conventional predictive modeling approaches. Data sparsity manifests through extreme class imbalance, with a ratio of 27:2:1 between the most common dismissal type (caught by fielder) and rare events such as stumpings or hit wickets. This imbalance creates difficulties for standard classification algorithms, which often fail to capture patterns in minority classes. High-dimensionality emerges from the extensive feature space required to represent player statistics across multiple contexts (career, venue, opponent, temporal), while temporal dependencies introduce non-stationarity as player form and match conditions evolve throughout careers and seasons.

These limitations motivate three core research questions that guide our investigation: 
\begin{itemize}
    \item[\textbf{RQ1}:] \emph{Which contextual inputs maximize the prediction accuracy of LLMs?}
    \item[\textbf{RQ2}:] \emph{How do different LLM variants compare in terms of prediction performance?}
    \item[\textbf{RQ3}:] \emph{What is the optimal trade-off between accuracy and cost for real-world applications?}
\end{itemize}

To rigorously address these research questions, we designed a comprehensive evaluation framework. Central to evaluating model performance (RQ2) and the accuracy-cost tradeoff (RQ3), we selected four distinct LLMs representing a spectrum of capability, cost, and architecture. Our selection includes a large, state-of-the-art model (GPT-4.1); two smaller, efficient variants from the same family (GPT-4.1-mini and GPT-4.1-nano); and o4-mini, a compact model distinguished by its strong reasoning capabilities.

This diverse portfolio allows us to analyze performance not only across different model scales but also between general-purpose and specialized reasoning architectures, providing a nuanced view of the accuracy-cost-capability tradeoff. To evaluate these models, we employ top-1 and top-2 accuracy, as cricket dismissal prediction often involves multiple plausible outcomes. Pairwise differences between models are tested with McNemar’s exact test \cite{mcnemar_significance_testing}. Our full factorial experimental design combines these four models with nine contextual input combinations, resulting in 36 unique configurations that provide a comprehensive exploration of the problem space.


\section{Methodology}

\subsection{Data Collection Pipeline}
\label{sec:data-collection}

We assembled a comprehensive dataset for dismissal prediction by scraping publicly available match statistics and commentary from ESPNcricinfo, the leading source for cricket data. All records were public sports data with no personal information, and collection adhered to the site’s robots.txt and rate‑limit guidelines.

Our data collection encompassed over 3,000 ODI matches spanning multiple years, capturing diverse playing conditions, rule changes, and evolutionary trends in cricket tactics. This temporal coverage ensures our models encounter the full spectrum of contextual variations that influence dismissal patterns across different eras of the sport. The dataset includes four primary data types: match scorecards detailing innings-by-innings performance, ball-by-ball commentary providing rich textual descriptions of match dynamics, detailed player statistics aggregated across multiple dimensions, and venue information capturing ground-specific characteristics that influence playing conditions.

We focused our analysis on seven international players representing diverse teams and playing styles: Babar Azam (Pakistan), Shai Hope (West Indies), Ibrahim Zadran (Afghanistan), Daryl Mitchell (New Zealand), Shubman Gill (India), Harry Tector (Ireland), and Charith Asalanka (Sri Lanka). These players were selected from the ICC top 10 ODI batsmen rankings as of 1st July 2025, with one player chosen from each country to ensure broad geographic diversity. This approach provides a balanced representation of seven cricketing nations and supports robust cross-context performance evaluation. The resulting dataset comprises over 1,700 processed commentary files, 600+ aggregated player datasets, 3,000+ match scorecards, and performance records from 100+ cricket grounds worldwide, providing the scale and diversity necessary for comprehensive LLM evaluation.


\subsection{Contextual Input Framework}
Building upon the heterogeneous input space defined in our problem formulation, we designed a systematic framework for organizing and evaluating different contextual information sources. Our approach distinguishes between structured contexts that provide quantitative performance metrics and unstructured contexts that capture rich descriptive information.

Structured contexts encompass four key dimensions that capture different aspects of player performance patterns. Career statistics provide key metrics including batting averages, strike rates, and historical dismissal patterns segmented by bowler types, establishing baseline performance expectations. Venue-specific performance records capture ground-wise statistical variations that reflect how players adapt to different pitch conditions, ground dimensions, and local playing environments. Opponent-specific analysis reveals team-wise historical performance patterns and matchup dynamics that influence tactical approaches and dismissal probabilities. Temporal patterns track recent form, seasonal variations, and career trajectory trends that capture evolving player capabilities and current performance states.

Ball-by-ball commentary offers real-time match descriptions and player behavior analysis that capture subtle performance indicators and situational dynamics~\cite{sports_text_analytics}. Match conditions encompass pitch reports, weather conditions, and venue characteristics that influence playing strategies and dismissal probabilities. Strategic context includes descriptions of field placements, bowling changes, and pressure situations that provide insights into tactical decision-making and its impact on dismissal outcomes.


\subsection{Ablation Study Design}
To systematically evaluate the contribution of different contextual inputs to prediction accuracy, we designed an ablation study that progressively introduces information sources while maintaining strict experimental controls. Our methodology evaluates nine distinct contextual input combinations across four LLM variants, resulting in 36 total configurations.

Our experimental approach follows a structured methodology that isolates the contribution of each information source. We start with a baseline configuration, where the LLM receives only basic match and player identification data, relying solely on its pre-trained knowledge. Then, we progressively add general career statistics, followed by venue-specific performance data, opponent-specific statistics, and, finally, textual commentary. This incremental progression allows us to precisely attribute accuracy improvements to specific contextual inputs. Throughout the experiment, players' identities and evaluation protocols remain constant across all configurations, ensuring that any observed effects can be attributed solely to the contextual inputs.

Reproducibility represents a key aspect of our methodology, implemented through a comprehensive automated experimental framework.
Our automated system ensures identical prompt structures, response parsing protocols, and evaluation metrics across all experimental conditions, eliminating potential sources of systematic bias.

Figure~\ref{fig:o4mini-ablation-configs} illustrates the complete spectrum of contextual input configurations evaluated in our ablation study, using o4-mini as an exemplar. The systematic progression demonstrates the methodological rigor underlying our approach: Random Classifier randomly selects one of eight dismissal modes with equal probability; Top Career Dismissal always predicts the most frequent dismissal type from a player's historical record; Baseline (All Off) provides the LLM with only basic match context and player identification, relying entirely on pre-trained knowledge; General Stats adds comprehensive career statistics including batting averages and dismissal patterns; General Stats + Venue Stats incorporates ground-specific performance history; General Stats + Venue Stats + Vs-Team Stats adds opponent-specific matchup data; Venue Stats + Vs-Team Stats combines venue and opponent contexts without general statistics; and configurations with Commentary integrate ball-by-ball textual descriptions. This comprehensive evaluation framework enables precise attribution of accuracy improvements to specific contextual inputs while maintaining experimental rigor.

\section{Experimental Setup}

\subsection{Model Selection and Configuration}
Our experimental framework centers on systematic evaluation of four leading LLM variants that represent different points along the accuracy-cost spectrum: GPT-4.1 (highest capability), GPT-4.1-mini (balanced performance), GPT-4.1-nano (efficiency-optimized), and o4-mini (reasoning-enhanced). This selection captures the diverse architectural approaches currently available for natural language understanding while ensuring comprehensive coverage of practical deployment scenarios ranging from resource-constrained environments to performance-critical applications.

We developed a prompt based on the chain-of-thought reasoning paradigm to guide LLMs through structured decision-making. Our approach includes analyzing career stats, venue patterns, opponent matchups, and commentary to generate consistent and interpretable dismissal predictions.

We conducted a computational cost analysis to inform practical deployment, tracking API expenses, inference latency, and resource needs across all model variants. This analysis allows us to directly compare accuracy-cost tradeoffs (see Table~\ref{tab:cost-comparison}), helping determine feasibility for real-world applications, from low-latency predictions in live match analysis to more resource-intensive post-match analytics where higher accuracy justifies the additional cost.


\subsection{Baseline Comparisons}
To establish the performance advantages of LLM-based approaches, we implemented a comprehensive suite of traditional machine learning models that represent the current state-of-practice in sports prediction. Our baseline evaluation includes Random Forest (achieving 43\% accuracy through ensemble decision-making), Naive Bayes (32\% accuracy via probabilistic classification), Logistic Regression (43\% accuracy through linear discriminative modeling), Support Vector Machine (43\% accuracy via margin-based classification), and XGBoost (43\% accuracy through gradient boosting). This diverse collection captures different algorithmic paradigms while providing robust comparison points for LLM performance evaluation.

Our traditional model evaluation employed 5-fold cross-validation to ensure robust performance estimation, revealing concerning patterns that underscore the limitations identified in our problem formulation. High variance in performance metrics (±4-6\% standard deviation) indicates unstable prediction behavior that reflects the fundamental challenges of data sparsity and class imbalance inherent in cricket dismissal prediction. These limitations manifest through poor generalization to new player-venue-opponent combinations and the inability to process textual context that provides crucial situational awareness for accurate dismissal prediction.


\subsection{Evaluation Protocol}

Our evaluation framework operationalizes the experimental design established in our methodology through systematic assessment of 501 dismissal instances per configuration, resulting in 18,036 total LLM evaluations across our complete experimental matrix of 36 configurations (4 models $\times$ 9 contextual input combinations). This scale ensures sufficient statistical power for robust conclusions while capturing the full diversity of contextual factors that influence dismissal prediction accuracy.

\section{Results}

\subsection{Main Findings}
Our comprehensive experimental evaluation reveals substantial improvements in cricket dismissal prediction accuracy when employing LLMs with optimal contextual inputs. The individual best configuration demonstrates a dramatic leap from 43\% (best performing traditional model) to 57.5\% top-1 accuracy, representing a 14.5 percentage point absolute improvement (Figure~\ref{fig:model-comparison}\subref{fig:top1-comparison}). This advancement translates to a 33.7\% relative enhancement over conventional approaches, meaning our LLM-based method improves upon traditional model performance by more than one-third. Across all 36 experimental configurations, LLMs achieve an average top-1 accuracy of 48.2\%, indicating consistent performance gains even in suboptimal contextual scenarios.

Top-2 accuracy reveals even larger improvements, with the best individual configuration reaching 79.2\% compared to the best performing traditional model reaching 59\% (Figure~\ref{fig:model-comparison}\subref{fig:top2-comparison}), while the overall LLM average across all configurations attains 67.4\% accuracy. This substantial enhancement in top-2 performance reflects the practical value of LLM-based approaches for real-world applications where providing ranked dismissal probabilities often proves more valuable than forcing single predictions.

The model hierarchy emerging from our systematic evaluation identifies o4-mini as the highest-performing variant, achieving peak accuracy of 57.5\% (top-1) and 79.2\% (top-2). This finding demonstrates that reasoning-enhanced architectures provide significant advantages for the complex contextual analysis required in cricket dismissal prediction. Statistical significance testing through McNemar's test confirms that differences between optimal contextual combinations and baseline configurations exceed conventional significance thresholds ($p$ < 0.01), establishing the reliability of observed improvements (See Section~\ref{sec:mcnemar-testing}).

Our ablation study reveals the contribution of each contextual input to prediction accuracy through progressive context addition. In Figure~\ref{fig:ablation-study}, we compare four key configurations that reveal the most significant trends across all models. Each group of four bars represents one model and includes the following configurations: a random classifier (-2), always predicting the most frequent career dismissal (-1), and the two best-performing configurations for that model (typically 6 and 7). The final two bars demonstrate substantial gains achieved through LLMs by combining structured and unstructured input. Beginning with the configuration of using top career dismissal alone (43.3\% top-1 accuracy), we observe substantial improvements as contextual richness increases. The addition of venue-specific context leads to 8\,pp improvement to 51.3\% accuracy, demonstrating that ground-specific factors, pitch conditions, dimensional characteristics, and playing patterns, provide crucial predictive signals that career averages alone cannot capture.

Commentary integration produces more nuanced results, with marginal gains that vary substantially across different LLM variants. While textual match descriptions provide rich contextual information, the inconsistent benefits suggest that current LLM architectures may not yet fully exploit unstructured textual inputs for numerical prediction tasks, particularly when high-quality structured contexts are already available.

Our context effectiveness ranking establishes a clear hierarchy: Venue + Opponent contexts consistently outperform individual Venue-only or Opponent-only configurations, which subsequently surpass approaches using Commentary alone across all tested models (see Figure~\ref{fig:ablation-study}). \subsection{Model Comparison Analysis}

Our evaluation across four LLM variants reveals a clear performance hierarchy that balances accuracy with practical deployment considerations. o4-mini achieves the highest performance with 57.5\% top-1 accuracy, demonstrating the advantages of reasoning-enhanced architectures for complex contextual analysis tasks. GPT-4.1-mini follows closely at 56.3\%, while GPT-4.1 achieves 55.3\% and GPT-4.1-nano reaches 53.5\%. This ranking reflects the expected relationship between model capability and predictive performance, with more sophisticated architectures consistently outperforming efficiency-optimized variants.

However, cost-accuracy tradeoff analysis reveals that GPT-4.1-mini offers the optimal balance for practical deployment scenarios (Table~\ref{tab:cost-comparison}). \subsection{Statistical Significance Testing}
\label{sec:mcnemar-testing}

To test whether the observed differences in performance between input configurations are meaningful or just due to chance, we perform pairwise statistical comparisons using McNemar’s test. Each configuration uses the same model on the same test set, allowing us to directly check if changing the input context leads to significantly different predictions. Statistical validation confirms the significance of our observed performance improvements across contextual configurations (Figure~\ref{fig:mcnemar-analysis}). Our pairwise comparison analysis evaluates all meaningful combinations of contextual inputs, enabling precise identification of which enhancements provide statistically reliable benefits versus those that may reflect random variation.

The most compelling finding emerges from comparing \emph{venue + opponent contexts} against the baseline career statistics configuration, where McNemar's test yields ($p$ < 0.001), providing overwhelming evidence for the significance of contextual enhancement. This result confirms that the observed 14.5\% absolute improvement represents a genuine advancement rather than statistical noise, supporting definitive conclusions about the value of situational context for dismissal prediction.

Commentary addition produces more nuanced statistical patterns, with non-significant differences in many pairwise comparisons reflecting the inconsistent benefits observed across different models. While some LLM variants show modest improvements when textual commentary supplements structured contexts, the lack of statistical significance indicates that these gains do not reliably generalize across all experimental conditions.

\section{Discussion}

\subsection{Why Contextual Inputs Matter}

Our empirical findings reveal that contextual factors operate as key drivers of dismissal prediction accuracy, with venue and opponent contexts providing the most substantial predictive value. Venue effects extend far beyond simple statistical averaging, encompassing complex interactions between ground dimensions, pitch characteristics, and local playing conditions that create distinctive dismissal patterns. Large venues with expansive boundaries favor caught dismissals in specific field regions, while smaller grounds with varying pitch compositions alter the relative likelihood of bowled versus LBW dismissals. Weather patterns, seasonal variations, and ground-specific maintenance practices introduce additional contextual layers that career statistics alone cannot capture.

Opponent strategies represent an equally crucial contextual dimension, reflecting the sophisticated tactical frameworks that modern cricket teams employ. Different opponents deploy distinctive bowling combinations, field placement philosophies, and pressure application strategies that systematically influence dismissal patterns. Our results demonstrate that team-wise historical matchups contain predictive signals that transcend individual player statistics, suggesting that strategic context operates at a higher analytical level than traditional performance metrics typically address.

The limited impact of commentary integration highlights key limitations in current text processing methods. While detailed ball-by-ball commentary is descriptive, its uneven usefulness across LLM variants suggests real-time textual data may introduce more noise than useful signal for numerical predictions. Currently, structured data offers more reliable predictions than unstructured text, although future improvements in multimodal LLMs may better leverage textual context.

Our empirical validation confirms that venue and opponent contexts capture crucial predictive signals that career statistics fundamentally miss. The 14.5\% absolute improvement achieved through contextual enhancement demonstrates that situational factors often dominate individual historical patterns in determining dismissal outcomes, validating the theoretical expectation that cricket represents a deeply contextual sport where environmental and strategic factors exert decisive influence.

\subsection{Traditional Model Limitations}

Our evaluation highlights structural limitations that prevent traditional ML approaches from achieving competitive performance on the dismissal prediction task. Data sparsity manifests not merely through the extreme 27:2:1 class imbalance ratio, but through the broader challenge of capturing meaningful patterns when rare dismissal types provide insufficient training examples for reliable statistical inference.

Scalability constraints manifest through the requirement for complete model retraining when adapting to new scenarios. Traditional approaches cannot gracefully incorporate new venues or opponents without extensive data collection and retraining cycles, making them unsuitable for dynamic sports environments where conditions constantly evolve. The operational complexity of maintaining multiple specialized libraries, extensive hyperparameter optimization, and domain-specific preprocessing pipelines creates additional barriers that limit practical deployment, particularly for applications requiring frequent updates or real-time prediction capabilities.


\subsection{LLM Advantages}

LLMs address the fundamental limitations of traditional approaches through several key architectural advantages that are particularly valuable for contextual sports prediction tasks. Natural context processing enables direct integration of textual match information without requiring manual feature engineering or information loss through quantization. LLMs can simultaneously process numerical statistics, venue descriptions, tactical commentary, and strategic insights within a unified framework, capturing the full richness of contextual information that human experts naturally consider when making dismissal predictions.

Unified frameworks mitigate infrastructure complexities common in traditional ML pipelines. Whereas conventional methods require specialized libraries, preprocessing, and domain-specific feature engineering, LLM-based systems employ simplified APIs to handle diverse inputs directly. This reduces deployment overhead and enhances flexibility in integrating new data sources, potentially benefiting production sports analytics environments, though further evaluation across practical scenarios is warranted.


\subsection{Implications for Sports Analytics}

Our findings establish important precedents for the broader application of LLMs to sports prediction tasks beyond cricket dismissal analysis. The contextual input evaluation framework we developed provides a systematic methodology for identifying which information sources maximize prediction accuracy across different sports domains~\cite{contextual_sports_modeling}. This approach proves particularly valuable for sports characterized by complex situational dynamics where traditional statistical approaches struggle to capture the full range of performance-influencing factors.

Real-world deployment opportunities span multiple commercial and analytical contexts where improved dismissal prediction provides immediate value. Broadcasting analysis benefits from more accurate commentary insights and real-time tactical explanations, while betting markets gain from enhanced predictive models that incorporate contextual factors often overlooked by traditional approaches. Coaching applications represent perhaps the most impactful opportunity, as improved understanding of contextual dismissal patterns enables more sophisticated tactical planning and strategic decision-making during matches.

Our methodological contributions extend beyond cricket-specific findings to establish best practices for LLM evaluation in sports analytics contexts. The systematic ablation study approach, rigorous statistical validation framework, and cost-accuracy tradeoff analysis (Table~\ref{tab:cost-comparison}) provide replicable methodologies for future sports prediction research. These contributions prove particularly valuable as the sports analytics community increasingly explores LLM applications across diverse prediction tasks and competitive formats.

Cost-accuracy considerations reveal important practical constraints that influence deployment decisions across different application scenarios. Our finding that GPT-4.1-mini provides optimal balance between predictive performance and operational costs establishes important precedents for production sports analytics systems. This analysis demonstrates the necessity of considering computational overhead alongside raw accuracy when evaluating LLM approaches for commercial deployment, particularly in applications requiring frequent predictions or real-time analysis capabilities.
